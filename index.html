For grown-ups

Small toys for big questions.

Poke & See is a shelf of tiny science toys for kids aged 3 to 5. Each one does one thing. Your kid pokes it, sees what happens, and then gets to go and do something else.

Why this exists

My son is curious about ice and boiling water. Most three-year-olds are. So I made him a page where he could slide a knob from cold to hot and watch ice melt into water, then float away as steam.

I wanted him to understand it with his hands. And then I wanted him to put the tablet down and go and look at a real ice cube.

That's the whole idea. One question, one small toy that answers it, a grown-up sitting next to them, and a reason to look at the real world afterwards.

Everything here starts from one idea: respect the kid.

Three-year-olds are serious scientists with small hands. We build for their minds, not their size.

Kids can handle real words

We say steam, molecules, shadow and pigment. Real numbers with real units, like 100°C. Simplified, but never wrong.

The real world is the show

Oceans look like oceans and leaves look like leaves. A water molecule is one oxygen and two hydrogens, not a blob with a smiley face.

Poke first, words later

Every toy does something the moment it's touched. Kids work it out with their hands, and the words catch up. No reading needed: everything important is spoken aloud.

Finding out is the reward

No stars, no points, no "Great job!". If your kid gasps when the ice melts, the toy has done its job.

Mindfully made, never addictive

Most apps for kids are built to keep them on the screen for as long as possible. We're building the opposite: toys that hand your kid back to you.

Every toy is small on purpose. There's nothing to collect and no next episode. Most kids find out what a toy does in a few minutes, and that's a good place to stop.

How to play together

You don't need to know the science. Each toy tells you what you need, and your kid will lead.

  1. Sit together, sound on

    A tablet on the table between you works best. The toys talk, so turn the volume up a little.

  2. Let them poke first

    Hold back the explaining. Watch what they try. Kids test things in orders a grown-up would never think of.

  3. Say the real word

    When the ice melts, say "melting". When the clouds appear, say "steam". The toy says it too, so they hear it twice.

  4. Ask one question

    Tap "For grown-ups" for the science in one sentence and one question to ask. Then wait for the answer. Wrong answers are good answers at three.

  5. Take it off screen

    Try the real-world twin below. The toy shows the idea; the kitchen, the garden and the bathtub make it stick.

  6. Put it away

    When they've found what it does, close it. It'll be exactly the same tomorrow, and so will their questions.
